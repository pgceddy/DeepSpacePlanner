Processing Notes

A working reference for taking Seestar S50 data from stack to finish, organized by what kind of target you pointed at. Siril-first, with PixInsight notes where it helps.

GALAXY

Small, faint galaxy

size < 5′ · mag > 10 — e.g. NGC 6340, most field galaxies near a brighter target

  1. Stack with Winsorized or Linear Fit Clipping rejection. Rejects outliers and satellite trails without discarding faint real signal.
  2. Run Background Extraction before any stretch. Sample points only in clean sky around the galaxy — never on or near the core.
  3. Photometric Color Calibration (PCC). Works well as long as the field has a reasonable star count.
  4. Stretch with Generalized Hyperbolic Stretch (GHS), not a single Asinh pass. Gives control to pull up the faint core without blowing out field stars.
  5. Denoise before sharpening. At this size and brightness you're fighting noise more than resolving detail.
  6. Crop in tight. The galaxy occupies a small fraction of the frame — cropping first makes every later stretch/curve decision easier.
  7. Optional: star reduction. Helps a small faint galaxy visually hold its own against the field instead of getting lost among stars.

GALAXY

Medium galaxy

size 5′–15′, moderate surface brightness — e.g. M94

  1. Standard calibrate → register → stack with Winsorized rejection.
  2. Background Extraction, sampling well outside the galaxy's outer disk.
  3. PCC for color, then a moderate GHS or Asinh stretch — you have more dynamic range to work with than a faint target, so you can afford a gentler curve.
  4. Sharpen the core selectively (star mask or luminance mask) once structure is visible, rather than sharpening the whole frame.
  5. Light denoise on the outer disk/halo, where signal thins out fastest.

GALAXY

Large galaxy or galaxy group

size > 15′, or a cluster of galaxies in one field — e.g. Markarian's Chain, Coma Cluster field

  1. Stack with generous frame count — large fields benefit from drizzle integration if you have the subs for it.
  2. Background Extraction with more sample points, spread around the full frame margin since gradients are more visible across a wider field.
  3. SPCC (Spectrophotometric Color Calibration) in PixInsight if available — more accurate across multiple galaxies of different types than single-target PCC.
  4. Stretch in stages: a first pass for the brightest cores, a second targeted pass for fainter group members, blended back together.
  5. Annotate before you crop. Worth running an Aladin/SIMBAD overlay pass to catch fainter cluster members or background quasars before you commit to a final frame.

NEBULA

Small nebula

size < 15′ — compact emission or reflection nebulae

  1. Stack with rejection tuned to preserve faint extended signal — avoid overly aggressive clipping.
  2. Background Extraction, being careful with sample placement since nebulosity can extend further than it first appears.
  3. Stretch with masking: protect the brightest core while pulling up faint outer wisps separately.
  4. Star reduction and/or masking for nebula enhancement — the technique you used on Ghost Nebula (VdB 141) applies directly here.
  5. Watch for mosaic seams if you've combined multiple Seestar sessions or panels.

NEBULA

Large diffuse nebula

size > 15′, low surface brightness — e.g. Sh2-216/232/235-type targets

  1. Longer integration matters more here than almost any other target type. Diffuse, low-surface-brightness nebulosity needs signal, not clever processing, to appear at all.
  2. Background Extraction is critical — large diffuse nebulae are easy to confuse with genuine gradients. Sample generously and check the extracted background model before subtracting.
  3. Stretch gently and in multiple passes rather than one aggressive curve — you're trying to reveal faint extended structure without crushing it into flat gray.
  4. Consider narrowband-style contrast boosts even from broadband OSC data (e.g. selective saturation on H-alpha-rich regions) if the Seestar's filter and target support it.
  5. Denoise before the final stretch pass, not after — noise gets amplified fastest in the shadow/midtone push these targets need.

NEBULA

Planetary nebula

small, high surface brightness, sharp-edged

  1. Stack tight — these targets are usually bright enough that shorter integrations still work; the challenge is resolution, not signal.
  2. Minimal background extraction needed — the field around a planetary nebula is usually clean.
  3. Aggressive but careful stretch on the shell, protecting the central star/core from clipping.
  4. Sharpen the shell edge specifically — this is one of the few target types where real sharpening (not just noise reduction) pays off at S50 resolution.
  5. Crop tight and consider a modest zoom/scale-up in post since the object itself is small in-frame.

CLUSTER

Open cluster

loose group of stars, wide range of brightness

  1. Shorter integration is often fine. Stars are point sources with good SNR relative to diffuse targets — don't over-integrate at the expense of a session on something fainter.
  2. Background Extraction is usually light-touch — clusters rarely have strong gradients unless embedded in nebulosity.
  3. PCC for accurate star colors — this is where color calibration actually shows, since the eye reads star color directly.
  4. Avoid heavy star reduction — the stars are the subject, not competition for it.
  5. Watch bright-star halos/blooming and manage with a gentle highlight rolloff rather than clipping.

CLUSTER

Globular cluster

dense, compact stellar core

  1. Protect the core from saturation during stack/stretch. Dense cores clip easily; a mask that limits the stretch there while pulling up the fainter outer stars separately makes a real difference.
  2. Background Extraction as usual, sampling outside the cluster's halo.
  3. Deconvolution (PixInsight) can help resolve core stars if seeing/tracking allowed sharp subs — otherwise skip it, it will just amplify noise.
  4. Stretch in two zones: a restrained curve for the core, a stronger one for the resolved outer stars, blended with a radial or luminance mask.

GENERAL

General / unsure what you're looking at

fallback workflow — works reasonably for most targets

  1. Calibrate, register, stack with Winsorized rejection.
  2. Background Extraction before any stretch.
  3. PCC for color calibration.
  4. GHS stretch, checking histogram carefully rather than trusting autostretch defaults.
  5. Denoise, then decide whether the target needs sharpening, star reduction, or neither — that call depends on what showed up once background and stretch are done.
